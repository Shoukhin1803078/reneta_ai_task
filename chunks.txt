----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEDICINE: Maxpro 20 mg
SECTION: 1. What Maxpro is and what it is used for

Maxpro contains esomeprazole, a proton pump inhibitor that reduces the
amount of acid produced in the stomach. It is used for:
chronic heartburn and the symptoms of gastro-oesophageal reflux disease
(GERD)
healing and long-term maintenance of erosive oesophagitis
duodenal and gastric ulcers
eradication of Helicobacter pylori (together with amoxicillin and
clarithromycin) in duodenal ulcer disease
Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome and acid-related dyspepsia

MEDICINE: Maxpro 20 mg
SECTION: 2. Before you take Maxpro

Do not take if you are hypersensitive to esomeprazole or any component of the
formulation.
A good symptomatic response does not rule out the presence of a gastric
malignancy; tell your doctor if symptoms persist.
Do not chew or crush the tablet/pellets.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEDICINE: Rolip 10 mg
SECTION: 1. What Rolip is and what it is used for

Rolip contains rosuvastatin, a statin that lowers cholesterol. It is used, alongside
a suitable diet, to treat lipid disorders — including primary hyperlipidaemia,
mixed dyslipidaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, Type III hyperlipoproteinaemia, and
familial hypercholesterolaemia. It also helps slow the progression of
atherosclerosis and reduce the risk of heart attack, stroke, and arterial
procedures in high-risk patients, and is used for heterozygous familial
hypercholesterolaemia in children aged 10–17 when diet alone is not enough.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEDICINE: Rolip 10 mg
SECTION: 2. Before you take Rolip

Do not take if you: are hypersensitive to any component; have active liver
disease (including unexplained, persistently raised liver enzymes); are
pregnant or may become pregnant; or are breast-feeding.
Tell your doctor before taking if you have factors that increase the risk of muscle
problems (myopathy) — being over 65, an under-treated underactive thyroid, or
kidney impairment. Stop and contact your doctor if you develop unexplained
muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness. Your doctor will check your liver with
blood tests before treatment, at 12 weeks, and periodically thereafter.
